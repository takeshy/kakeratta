## Inspiration

Smartphones made it effortless to capture notes and photos, so we now accumulate far more personal data than we ever revisit. We forget we wrote something, or find it months later and wonder what it meant.

I first tried building an app that used AI to organize notes automatically. It didn't work as I hoped. It felt like having someone else clean your room: tidier, but you no longer know where anything is. When I had moved things around myself, I kept a rough sense of where they were. Yet once there is enough information, leaving it scattered means some of it is simply lost.

There was also an everyday frustration. We don't bother taking notes about everyday items we always bring with us, yet we still forget them. Even when the weather forecast warns of rain later, if it is sunny outside when leaving the house, we easily forget to bring an umbrella. What if, right before stepping out, an assistant could check your routine belongings, or remind you that rain is coming later and suggest taking an umbrella? In classic television dramas, wives would often give their husbands a gentle, caring reminder by the door before they headed out. I wanted that kind of timely, thoughtful nudge in daily life.

So I reframed the problem. Instead of expecting the user to search, what if the organizer itself could say "You mentioned this before" when something forgotten became relevant again? And what if, for memories unused for a long time, it could ask "Can I forget this now?"

There was also a personal reason. One of my biggest regrets is losing touch with someone who had always cared about me. I was busy, forgot to send a thank-you, and by the time I noticed, too much time had passed. Relationships are made of small fragments too: "I should tell them this next time." "They might like this as a gift." "I haven't talked to them in a while." Those disappear as easily as notes.

That is how kakeratta was born.

## What it does

kakeratta is a personal AI timeline. You post fragments of daily life (text, photos, URLs, voice), and AI personas dedicated to specific interests or people remember only what is relevant to them.

The system is organized around four clear responsibilities:
- **Timeline** = What happened (an objective, chronological record of your daily thoughts, activities, and media).
- **Memory** = What you want the AI to remember (structured facts, routines, and interests curated by dedicated personas).
- **Favorites** = AI artifacts you want to keep (infographics, summaries, news analyses, or recommendations saved with one tap).
- **Search** = A way to dig up past Timeline entries (hybrid keyword and semantic retrieval across forgotten moments).

An example. On a trip to Shizuoka, you mention a restaurant you wanted to try but didn't have time for. A travel persona quietly remembers it. Weeks later you post "Going to Shizuoka tomorrow." During its daily check-in, the persona connects the new post with the old memory:

"You didn't make it to that restaurant last time. Maybe this trip?"

You would never have searched for it, because you had forgotten it existed. Search can't find what you've forgotten exists. kakeratta brings it back.

### Fragments go only to the personas they concern

kakeratta ships with built-in personas designed for everyday life: `tabe` (food and dining), `shiori` (books), `sona` (routine belongings, outing preparation, weather forecasts, and timely follow-ups), `support` (product specifications and user assistance), and `infographic` (transforming notes into visual HTML infographics). Users can also create personas for specific topics or people who matter to them.

Each interest persona has an explicit, readable routing condition, and a post goes only to the personas whose conditions it matches. Person personas skip the router; they receive a post when it contains that person's name, nickname, or a registered keyword.

A persona that receives a post decides whether to remember, reply, use a tool, schedule a follow-up, or do nothing. Silence is the normal case. If every AI commented on every post, the timeline would drown. You can always call a persona directly with @.

### Timely preparation and doorstep reminders: sona

`sona` brings that classic doorstep care into everyday life. It tracks your usual belongings, habits, and past forgotten items.

When you mention an upcoming outing, `sona` schedules a timely preparation check shortly before departure. It inspects the local weather forecast via WeatherAPI—recognizing when clear skies now will turn to rain later—and sends a Web Push notification: "It's sunny right now, but rain is expected around 3 PM. Remember to bring your umbrella, and do you have your keys?"

It does not nag you with rigid calendar alarms hours in advance. Instead, like a thoughtful family member seeing you off by the door, it gives you a timely, gentle nudge when it actually matters.

### Answer-only personas: memory is optional

Not every AI needs to remember everything. In fact, forcing every assistant to build memory creates clutter, unwanted comments, and unnecessary costs.

kakeratta allows personas to be configured as **answer-only** (`memoryEnabled: false`). These personas specialize purely in on-demand analysis, explanation, and creation without saving private notes:
- **Infographics (`infographic`)**: Generating infographics is one of the most common and effective everyday AI use cases for my peers and myself. This persona transforms lengthy articles, complex topics, or timeline fragments into clean, standalone, responsive HTML/SVG infographics (powered by Nemotron 3 Ultra). The results render safely in an isolated frame, can be saved to Favorites, or exported directly to Bluesky as images.
- **News and Explanations**: With date-filtered web search via Tavily, personas can research breaking news and provide deep, grounded background explanations without accumulating conversational baggage.
- **Product Support (`support`)**: Answers questions by referencing the system's OKF documentation directly, remaining strictly factual without retaining personal memory.

By bypassing note-keeping, summary rewriting, and action review phases for memoryless personas, kakeratta cuts inference tokens and latency substantially—saving operational costs while eliminating the annoyance of unsolicited memory accumulation and unprompted daily check-ins.

### Favorites: keeping what matters

When an AI persona delivers a valuable artifact—an insightful infographic, a news breakdown, or a curated list of recommendations—you can save it directly to **Favorites** with a single click. Favorites can be filtered by persona or searched separately, ensuring your favorite AI deliverables stay organized and accessible without disappearing into the flowing daily Timeline.

### Personas are created and refined in plain language

"Remember the wines I drink and help me choose one next time."

That is a complete wine persona. Later: "Keep replies shorter." "Remember why I liked it, not just the label."

A person persona works the same way:

"My partner Nagomi was born November 10, 1972. She loves K-pop and cosmetics, plays piano, and likes spicy Thai food."

From then on, Nagomi's persona collects related fragments. It can remind you before her birthday, remember past gifts, and suggest ideas at the right moment.

### Bringing things back, and letting them go

- **Daily check-in.** Once a day, each active persona on a daily schedule reviews its memory alongside recent timeline activity, including posts never routed to it. A quieter mode speaks only when the user needs to decide something.
- **Monthly look-back for people.** Once a month, kakeratta summarizes the timeline once into an editable This month post, with each line linked to its source. Each person persona on a monthly schedule reads that summary plus the posts mentioning its person, surfacing things like "You haven't talked in a while" or "You said you'd do this together someday."
- **Scheduled preparation and follow-ups.** `sona` checks upcoming plans and preparation shortly before departure—comparing belongings and checking live WeatherAPI forecasts—and follows up on chores shortly after the promised moment ("Did you get the flowers?").
- **Memories have an ending.** Notes keep what you said, summarized into concise field facts alongside dated records. Candidate lists a persona produced from searches expire automatically if you don't react. User-derived memories get review dates where the persona asks whether to keep, postpone, or forget. User-derived memories are never deleted automatically.

All of this runs server-side, so the browser can be closed.

### Capture a fragment even without a connection

After opening kakeratta online once, you can reopen it offline, see recently cached conversations, and save text, photos, URLs, and replies on the device. Pending posts are sent automatically when connectivity and authentication return while the app is open.

### Nothing is learned behind your back

Every memory can be inspected, traced to the post that created it, corrected, or forgotten without deleting the original post.

Routing and replies can be corrected too. For any post, you can see which personas were called and why others weren't. Say "This persona should have received it," and the AI rewrites that persona's routing condition and retries the same post. For person personas, it proposes new keywords, or you choose which matched keyword to remove. Say "No recommendations, just one line on how it differs from last time," and only the reply behavior changes. The retried reply stays off the timeline until you approve it.

You judge the result.

## How we built it

Unstructured human timeline → personas for interests or people → separate structured memory (or pure answer generation for answer-only personas)

### NVIDIA Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory

Models are assigned by task difficulty and frequency:

- **Nemotron 3 Ultra:** drafting personas, deeper answers when requested, and visual HTML infographic generation (`alwaysUltra: true`)
- **Nemotron 3 Super:** routing, normal persona execution, and workflow review
- **Nemotron 3.5 Lightning:** deciding whether to escalate to Ultra, and polishing dictated text
- **GLM-5.3-Flash:** understanding photos and screenshots
- **Qwen3-Embedding-8B:** semantic search over past fragments

Because Token Factory provides large, medium, and lightweight models, along with vision and embeddings, through one platform, I could repeatedly reassign model responsibilities without changing the provider integration. That mattered most for routing, which runs on every post.

In a September 19 replay of 20 real posts, with thinking enabled for all three models:

| Model | Estimated cost / routing call | Mean latency | Matches vs. post-hoc reference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Super | $0.00065 | 1.91 s | 17/20 |
| Ultra | $0.00300 | 2.28 s | 19/20 |
| Lightning | $0.00047 | 6.55 s | 17/20 |

Super cost about 22% as much as Ultra, while matching the post-hoc reference on 17 of 20 posts. All three of Super's mismatches concerned whether `plana` should also receive the post. Lightning was cheaper still, but substantially slower with thinking enabled.

That tradeoff made Super the default router. The cost estimates use recorded token usage and the experiment's prices and cover routing only. This was a small, post-hoc replay, not a general accuracy benchmark.

### Persona memory: field summaries, kakera records, and answer-only bypass

Long-term memory uses OKF (Open Knowledge Format) principles, refined into two lightweight, inspection-friendly structures per persona:
- **Field summaries (`{Field}.md`):** Concise, user-grounded factual summaries (capped at 2,000 characters) maintained by the background note keeper.
- **Kakera records (`records/YYYY-MM.md`):** One dated record line per relevant post with a short sentence, tags, and review/expiration dates.

For personas configured as answer-only (`memoryEnabled: false`), this entire memory pipeline is bypassed. The model answers the prompt directly; note-keeper writes, summary rewrites, and post-answer review calls are omitted. This cuts inference cost and latency while eliminating unwanted memory clutter.

The original timeline remains independent and searchable by keyword and embedding, even when no persona remembered a post. What a persona remembers, what artifacts you save to Favorites, and what actually happened on the Timeline are deliberately kept apart.

### Offline capture with a service worker

A service worker caches the application shell so the app can open without a connection. IndexedDB stores the latest 20 original posts and their replies per user, plus a separate outbox for unsent posts and images. The outbox is independent of that 20-post cache limit. Retries reuse the same post ID so a dropped connection does not create duplicate posts. Synchronization runs while the app is open; AI responses and image or URL analysis begin after the server receives the post. Clearing browser data removes anything that has not yet been sent.

### An MCP server for external planners: NemoClaw and Hermes

kakeratta also acts as an MCP server, allowing a persona's research and answer to be delegated to an external agent. I ran Hermes Agent inside NVIDIA NemoClaw / OpenShell, with Nemotron inference through Nebius Token Factory, and verified the full cycle against the production app: a user post opened a planning request, Hermes claimed it, read the persona's context, performed lookups, and submitted a workflow. kakeratta validated and reviewed that workflow, then executed it as the persona, including memory updates and a reply in the original thread. Since that run, the protocol has been narrowed: Hermes now submits only the answer, and kakeratta decides the schedules, actions and notes itself, one phase at a time, and assembles them with the answer into the workflow it checks and runs before publishing it. The narrower protocol is covered by automated tests but has not yet been re-verified end to end in NemoClaw.

The responsibility boundary stays the same whether the planner is built in or external: Hermes researches and answers; kakeratta decides and controls every change. Each persona can prefer the external planner, with the built-in model taking over if the agent is away or misses its deadline. A Wake Gate script checks for pending requests every minute without invoking an LLM when there is no work. This end-to-end flow completed autonomously; the reproduction steps and operational findings are documented in the [NemoClaw / OpenShell integration report](https://github.com/takeshy/kakeratta/blob/main/docs/nemoclaw-hermes-integration.md).

### Other design choices

- **Images are analyzed once.** The text representation is shared with every relevant persona. The tradeoff: if the first analysis misses something, no persona can revisit the image from another angle.
- **Tools belong to personas.** Each persona declares its own tools: web search with publication-date filters (Tavily), weather forecasts (WeatherAPI for outing preparation), place search (OpenStreetMap / Overpass / Nominatim), routes (OSRM), MCP-provided tools, and timeline/memory search.
- **Doorstep delivery via Web Push.** Scheduled reminders and preparation alerts arrive directly on the user's mobile device through Web Push, reaching them right before they leave.
- **Isolated rendering and image export.** HTML infographics run in an isolated browser frame without external scripts or network access, and can be converted client-side into images to share directly to Bluesky.

### Data handling

In the hosted app, timelines, persona memories, favorites, settings, and search vectors are stored in Firestore; images are stored in a private Cloud Storage bucket. Firebase Authentication identifies each user, and the Go API scopes access to that user's workspace.

The backend processes the data, sending relevant text, images, and embedding inputs to the configured inference provider, and queries to the tools a persona uses. An external agent can read the user's timeline and persona notes if the user enables that connection.

This is server-side processing, not end-to-end encryption: authorized infrastructure administrators can access stored data. Users can export or delete their workspace; self-hosted local mode stores it on disk.

## Challenges we ran into

The hardest problem was keeping internal complexity from becoming the user's problem. Under the hood are routing, model tiers, OKF, semantic search, MCP, phased planning, Web Push, and scheduled execution. The user should only need two rules:

Post what's on your mind. Tell a persona what you want in plain language.

So there is one search box: "Pinot" and "that light red I liked before" both start there, with keyword and semantic search combined behind it. We established four clear, intuitive boundaries: **Timeline** (what happened), **Memory** (what AI remembers), **Favorites** (the AI artifacts you want to keep), and **Search** (digging through past timeline entries). When routing or a reply is wrong, the user explains what they expected instead of editing rules. The inside became more complex so the outside could become simpler.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of

**Raising answer quality by narrowing what each AI call carries.** Instead of one enormous memory for everything about a user, the food persona remembers food, the books persona remembers reading, and each person in the user's life has a persona of their own. Each memory stays small enough to inspect and correct, while the timeline remains the full record of what actually happened.

Repeated tuning showed that the biggest gains in answer quality came from changing what each model call had to carry.

- **Context.** A persona is given its own index, the post, and the remarks under it, not the whole timeline, and reads further notes only when it needs them.
- **Notes.** Notes keep what the user said, not what an answer happened to research. A candidate list a persona produced expires automatically unless the user reacts to it, so memory does not fill with the persona's own guesses.
- **Workflow.** Each turn is split into phases that build one workflow. The persona first writes only its answer, with no note format or change schema in its prompt; separate phases then add schedules, actions, and notes. When one call had to do everything, it shortened its replies to make room for the notes it was required to write. With the answer on its own, the model can spend its attention on what the user actually asked.
- **Answer-only efficiency.** By allowing personas to be configured as memoryless (`memoryEnabled: false`), personas focused on tasks like infographics, news commentary, or technical support skip note keeping, summary rewriting, and action reviews entirely. This drastically reduces token costs and execution latency, while completely eliminating the annoyance of unwanted AI notes or unprompted check-ins.

Building this changed how I think about context in personal AI. It is not only about retrieving the right information, but about deciding what belongs together, what stays separate, which AI may remember what, and who gets to correct those decisions.

**Handing the answer to an external agent without handing over control.** I verified Hermes Agent inside NVIDIA NemoClaw / OpenShell, with inference routed to Token Factory, working against kakeratta's production MCP server. A persona can hand its research and answer to such an agent, so users can bring their own model, tools, and resources to shape how a persona thinks. The agent submits only the answer; kakeratta still decides what to schedule, act on, and remember, checks those changes, and runs them itself.

## What we learned

Organizing isn't enough. People remember by organizing, such as finding an old task while clearing a desk. Perfect organization by someone else can make things harder to find. Semantic search helps with vague recall, but only proactive resurfacing reaches what you've forgotten entirely.

Memory needs an ending. An early rule required personas to save something whenever they spoke. Soon tabe's memory was full of restaurants it had recommended, and memory became a record of the AI's own answers instead of the user. Giving memories a lifespan fixed this better than stricter save rules. Expiration became not the moment of deletion, but the last chance for information to return to the user before it's forgotten.

And sometimes, **not remembering is the best choice.** Not every task benefits from permanent memory. Specialized answer-only personas—like visual infographic creators and fresh-news explainers—deliver high value without the overhead or annoyance of maintaining memory.

Narrow the task before scaling the model. Early routing asked a large model "Which personas is this related to?" Reframing it as "Does this post match this explicit condition?" made routing practical on a mid-size model, and made the conditions readable and correctable. The same principle shaped the whole system: separate routing from execution, personas from memory, and limit each persona's tools. Defining what the AI is allowed to decide matters as much as making it more capable.

When to remind matters as much as what to remember, and so does when to forget. Timely preparation—like checking belongings and rain forecasts right before stepping out the door—turns AI from a distant database into a thoughtful daily companion.

## What's next for kakeratta

- **User-registered MCP servers and tools** that can be attached to any persona.
- **A community persona repository.** A persona is more than a prompt: routing conditions or keywords, behavior, memory structure, and tools. Packaged together, personas could be shared like apps, for example one that researches AI news every morning and explains the background that makes it matter.

The goal is a place where you toss in fragments of everyday life, and different AIs help you remember what matters, bring it back when it becomes relevant, and let it go when it no longer needs remembering.
