## Inspiration

I talk to myself a lot. I say things out loud about a meal I just had, something I noticed, or an interesting idea that came to mind. It feels like leaving a hook that might help me remember it later.

But in the end, I still forget.

I take notes too, but I hardly ever read them back. The things I do look up are usually things I already know exist, such as my calendar or contacts.

Even if an AI organized every note perfectly, that would not solve this problem. If I have forgotten that the information exists at all, I cannot even search for it. Something that felt trivial when I recorded it may become important a few weeks later.

So the first thing I wanted was not an AI that organizes things, but an AI that brings forgotten things back when they become relevant.

But talking to yourself has another purpose besides leaving memories.

Putting a vague thought into words can help organize it in your head. Saying a small discovery out loud can also make the excitement feel more real.

The problem is that ordinary self-talk has no one on the other side.

If I talk through an idea, sometimes I want someone to question it: "Is that really true?" If I found something exciting, I want someone to be excited with me. Sometimes I want a different point of view that I would not have reached on my own.

At the same time, I do not want an AI to reply to every unimportant thought. Sometimes I just want it to listen and remember.

So what I wanted was not simply a note-taking app for self-talk. I wanted something that remembers what I say, reacts when a reaction is useful, stays quiet when it has nothing to add, and can bring something back much later when it becomes relevant.

That led me to build a place where I can drop fragments of everyday life without first deciding whether they are important or how they should be organized.

Depending on the fragment, the AI may empathize, criticize an idea, remember something, or simply stay quiet. Later, when it becomes useful, it can look through past records or the web and speak up again.

That is kakeratta.

## What it does

kakeratta is a personal AI timeline where multiple subject-specific AIs decide which fragments of your everyday life matter to them and build separate memories from them.

Instead of sending everything you say into one large personal memory, kakeratta lets you simply drop in text, photos, URLs, and speech. Each AI persona receives only the fragments relevant to its role, then decides whether to remember something, reply, use a tool, schedule a later follow-up, or simply stay quiet.

Four **personas** are included by default:

- **tabe**: food
- **michi**: travel and going out
- **shiori**: reading
- **plana**: things that are too small for the calendar but still easy to forget

Each persona has its own memory and routing condition, so not every fragment is sent to every AI.

Doing nothing is important too. If every AI reacted to every post, the timeline would quickly become annoying. In kakeratta, it is normal for a persona to remember something without saying anything.

Personas can be created and edited in natural language.

For example:

> Remember the wines I drink and help me choose next time.

That is enough to create one.

While using it, I can also say things like:

> Reply more briefly.

> Remember why I liked the wine, not just the label.

and adjust how it behaves and what it remembers.

### Memory can be inspected and edited

A persona's long-term memory is not a black box.

You can see what it remembers and trace a memory back to the post or conversation that produced it.

Incorrect information can be fixed, and a specific memory can be deleted without removing the original post.

You can also see why the router did not call a particular persona.

If the routing was not what you expected, you can simply say something like:

> I wanted this persona to receive this too.

The AI rewrites the routing condition. After you approve and save it, kakeratta checks the same post again. If a previously missed persona is selected, it takes its normal turn on that original post, adding its reply and memory updates.

Replies work the same way. If a reply is not what you wanted, you can explain what you expected, and the AI rewrites the persona's response behavior and tries it on the same post. When you accept the improved reply, its exact text and note changes are applied to the original thread.

### It can speak up without being asked

Each persona can enable a **Daily check-in**. It is off by default.

When enabled, the persona wakes once a day and looks through its own memory and the recent timeline. It can also check recent posts that were not originally routed to it, searching for connections that only become relevant in light of what it already remembers.

For example, michi might remember a shop I wanted to visit on an earlier trip to Shizuoka. Weeks later, I might casually mention somewhere else on the timeline that I am going to Shizuoka tomorrow. Even if that new post was not originally meant for michi, its Daily check-in can connect the new trip with the old memory and say:

> We did not have time to visit that shop on your last trip to Shizuoka. If you are going there tomorrow, maybe you could stop by this time.

This can bring back something I had completely forgotten, without requiring me to remember that it exists and search for it myself.

The persona can use web search or tools when necessary, but it speaks only when it thinks there is something worth saying. If there is nothing useful, nothing happens.

Personas can also schedule simpler follow-ups.

For example, plana can remember:

> Buy flowers on the way home.

and ask later in the evening:

> Did you get the flowers?

Speech input is designed to make it possible to capture a thought as quickly as possible. Instead of repeatedly pressing buttons, you can finish a post by speaking a send phrase of your own choosing.

## How we built it

When a fragment is posted, kakeratta first uses a router to decide which personas should receive it.

Each selected persona can read its own memory and the past timeline, use tools when necessary, and decide whether to remember something, reply, follow up later, or stay silent.

At a high level, the structure is:

**mixed human timeline → subject-specific personas → structured memory for each persona**

### Nebius Token Factory and NVIDIA Nemotron

AI inference runs through Nebius Token Factory.

Rather than sending everything to a single large model, kakeratta assigns different models to different roles based on how often they run and how costly their mistakes would be.

- **Nemotron 3 Ultra**: persona creation and deeper or corrective answers when explicitly requested
- **Nemotron 3 Super**: routing, normal persona turns, and workflow review
- **Nemotron 3.5 Lightning**: judging whether a reply asks for a deeper answer; dictation polishing
- **GLM-5.3-Flash**: image understanding
- **Qwen3-Embedding-8B**: semantic search

Every persona normally runs on Super. Ultra is used when the user explicitly asks a persona to reconsider or go deeper.

Because these models are available through the same Token Factory API, I was able to move responsibilities between them repeatedly during development without rebuilding the surrounding integration.

### Persona memory with OKF

Long-term memory uses **OKF (Open Knowledge Format)**.

Memory is separated first by persona and then by topic. A persona does not need to receive an ever-growing history every time it runs. It can read and write only the notes it needs for the current task.

The notes contain Markdown-style tags and internal links. A persona can search related notes by tag or follow links to gather nearby information.

The full timeline is stored separately from memory.

A fragment does not disappear just because no persona decided it was worth remembering. It can still be found later through keyword search or semantic search using Qwen3-Embedding-8B.

What a persona remembers and everything that has happened in the timeline are intentionally not the same thing.

### Images are interpreted only once

Photos and screenshots are interpreted once by GLM-5.3-Flash.

The resulting text is then shared with the relevant personas instead of sending the same image through a vision model again for every persona.

There is a trade-off: if the first interpretation misses something, another persona cannot later inspect the original image from a different point of view.

### Tools belong to personas

A persona can combine personal memory with outside information.

However, I do not give every tool to every persona. The tools a persona can use are explicitly declared as part of its skill.

Current tools include:

- web search through Tavily
- place search through Geo MCP
- walking, cycling, and driving route search
- timeline and memory search

I built Geo MCP myself. In supported regions, it uses OpenStreetMap data and OSRM that I operate myself, with fallbacks depending on the configuration.

For example, michi can remember a place I wanted to visit and later check the travel time from my current location.

## Challenges we ran into

The main challenge was the number of features.

Internally, kakeratta uses routing, multiple model tiers, MCP, OKF, semantic search, workflows, and scheduled execution. If all of those concepts have to be explained to users, the app starts to feel like something they need to learn before they can use it.

But I wanted users to need to remember only two things:

- post whatever comes to mind
- tell a persona what you want it to do in ordinary language

So I tried to keep the underlying machinery out of the way.

For example, there is only one search box. Entering a word performs keyword search and then offers a "search by similar meaning" option. Entering a sentence automatically uses semantic search.

Persona adjustments follow the same idea.

If routing or a reply is not what you expected, you do not have to edit the underlying rules directly. You can explain what you wanted in ordinary language. The AI rewrites the routing condition or response behavior, and the saved change is tried on the same post. A newly selected persona answers the original post; when you accept an improved reply, its exact text and note changes are applied to the original thread.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of

The accomplishment I am most proud of is finding a practical way to avoid building one enormous memory about a person.

**unorganized human timeline → topic-specific personas → structured memory for each persona**

Personas make it possible to naturally separate messy personal data by topic. Each persona builds a smaller, subject-specific memory that can be inspected and corrected by the user.

Each persona can navigate that memory hierarchically with OKF, search by tags, and follow internal links, so it only needs to read and write information relevant to its current task.

The original timeline remains separate as the broader record, so what an AI chooses to remember does not become the only record of what happened.

For personal AI, I found that managing context is not only about retrieving the right information. It is also about deciding what belongs together, what should stay separate, and what the AI should be allowed to remember.

kakeratta's persona-based memory gives me a practical way to keep that context small, separated by subject, and visible and correctable by the user.

## What we learned

Simply having AI organize information is not enough.

People often remember things while organizing them. Cleaning up papers on a desk can remind you of something. Finding old photos while moving can start a conversation about family memories.

The opposite can also happen. If someone else tidies your room, things may actually become harder to find. Even if the room was messy before, you had your own sense of where things were.

For the cases where I actively want to find something, I added semantic search. Even if I cannot remember the exact words, I can search using a vague description.

But if I have forgotten that something exists at all, there is no reason to start searching for it.

That is why Daily check-in became important. A persona can revisit recent activity in the context of its own older memories and surface a connection that I would never have thought to search for myself.

Memory also needs a way to end. The rule that a persona must leave a note whenever it speaks had a side effect: when the food persona searched and proposed three restaurants, it filed those three restaurants, and its memory slowly filled with its own answers. Rather than judging better what to keep, I gave notes lifetimes. A note a persona made from its own searching expires the next day and is deleted by the application, unless the user reacts to it. A note about something the user said can carry a review date the persona chooses from the content, a few weeks for a shop to try, a few months for a topic. When that day comes, the persona asks in a short post whether to keep it, and the user's answer keeps, extends, or forgets the note. The application never deletes those on its own.

That turns expiry into the last moment something is brought back to the user, rather than the moment it is thrown away.

What I learned is that **when to bring something back matters just as much as what to remember**.

## What's next for kakeratta

At the moment, the operator provides MCP servers and tools and can assign a subset of them to each persona.

In the future, I want users to be able to register their own MCP servers and tools.

I also want to build a community repository for personas.

A persona is more than a prompt. It has a routing condition, behavior, memory structure, and tools. If those pieces can be packaged together, useful personas could be shared much like applications.

For example, someone could install a persona that investigates AI news every morning and explains not just what happened, but the background behind it.
